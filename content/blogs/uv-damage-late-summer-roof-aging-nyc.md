By late August, New York City roofs have absorbed months of near-constant summer sun. Unlike a storm or a leak, UV damage doesn't announce itself with a single dramatic event — it works quietly, breaking down roofing materials day after day until the effects finally become visible.

At **Neat Services Corp**, this is the time of year we start seeing the cumulative effects of a long, sunny summer: brittle shingle edges, faded coloring, and granule loss that homeowners didn't notice happening gradually.

## How UV Rays Break Down a Roof

Asphalt shingles are built to handle sun exposure, but that protection wears down over time. UV rays dry out the oils that keep shingles flexible, making them more prone to cracking and curling. Roofs facing south or west, which catch the most direct afternoon sun, tend to show this wear first.

Granule loss is another common late-summer issue. Those granules aren't just cosmetic — they shield the asphalt layer underneath from the sun. Once a shingle starts shedding granules, it ages faster with every week of exposure that follows.

## 3 Signs of UV Wear to Look For

1. **Faded or blotchy coloring:** uneven fading, especially on south- and west-facing slopes, is a sign the protective coating is breaking down.
2. **Granules collecting in gutters:** a small amount is normal, but a steady buildup points to accelerated aging.
3. **Brittle or curling shingle edges:** shingles that feel stiff or lift slightly at the corners have lost much of their flexibility.

## Why It's Worth Checking Now

UV damage compounds. A roof that's already brittle from summer sun is far more vulnerable to the wind and rain that come with fall storms and, eventually, the freeze-thaw cycles of winter. Catching wear now — while the weather is still dry and roofers can work comfortably — makes it much easier to address before the next season adds new stress.
