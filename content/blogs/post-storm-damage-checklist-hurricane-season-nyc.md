Mid-August through October is when New York City sees the most activity from hurricane season, and even storms that never make direct landfall can leave their mark. High winds, wind-driven rain, and flying debris put real stress on roofing and siding, and the damage isn't always obvious from the ground.

At **Neat Services Corp**, the days right after a storm are some of our busiest. Homeowners call about a leak that appeared overnight, a siding panel hanging loose, or debris scattered across the yard — signs that the exterior took a hit even if nothing looked wrong before the storm.

## Why Post-Storm Inspections Matter

Wind doesn't need to be catastrophic to cause damage. Gusts can lift shingle edges, loosen fasteners, and drive rain sideways into seams that are normally watertight. Once that seal is broken, it often takes another rainfall — sometimes weeks later — before a leak actually shows up inside the house. By then, water may have already been working its way into the roof deck or wall cavity.

Siding faces similar risk. Panels rated for high winds can still shift or crack if debris strikes them directly, and once a panel is compromised, it no longer protects the layers underneath from moisture.

## 4 Things to Check After Every Storm

1. **Missing or lifted shingles:** look for bare patches, curled edges, or shingles that seem out of alignment compared to the rest of the roof.
2. **Loose or cracked siding panels:** run your eyes along the exterior for panels that look bowed, cracked, or not flush with the wall.
3. **Debris in gutters and downspouts:** branches and granules can block drainage right when you need it most, during the next round of rain.
4. **New stains on ceilings or walls:** even a faint discoloration can be the first sign of a leak that started during the storm.

## Don't Wait for a Second Storm to Find Out

Hurricane season in the Northeast rarely means just one storm. If your roof or siding took stress from the last system, it's more vulnerable to the next one. Catching and fixing damage between storms is far less costly than dealing with a full leak or blow-off during the following event.
